\chapter{Discussion}

This chapter discusses the results obtained from the experiments on hallucination detection and mitigation, with focus on transferability across models, datasets, and representation layers.

\section{Hallucination Detection}

\subsection{Comparison Between Methods}
The updated results show a clear hierarchy among transfer approaches.

On \textbf{BeliefBankFacts} and \textbf{BeliefBankConstraints}, \textit{One-For-All} is the strongest method overall. It reaches very high tester performance in both transfer directions (BBF: up to 0.9628 AccTe and 0.9905 AUROC; BBC: up to 0.9947 AccTe and 0.9998 AUROC), largely outperforming classical alignment methods.

However, the same dominance does not hold uniformly on \textbf{HaluEval}. In the Gemma$\rightarrow$Llama direction, the best results are obtained by \textit{RidgeRegressor} (up to 0.6430 AccTe), while \textit{One-For-All} is notably weaker (0.546--0.567 AccTe). In the opposite direction (Llama$\rightarrow$Gemma), \textit{One-For-All} becomes the best approach (0.718--0.741 AccTe, up to 0.8172 AUROC). 

Among the other methods, \textit{CCA} and \textit{ReducedNonLinear} are generally the least robust, often approaching random-level performance on HaluEval and under difficult transfer settings.

\subsection{Dataset and Directional Analysis}
The three datasets now show a clearer separation in difficulty:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{BeliefBankConstraints (BBC)}: easiest setting for transferred detection, especially with One-For-All (near-perfect in both directions). Classical approaches are strong only in some directions (e.g., Gemma$\rightarrow$Llama with RidgeRegressor), but degrade substantially in the reverse direction.
    \item \textbf{BeliefBankFacts (BBF)}: still favorable for transfer, but with larger method-dependent variability than previously assumed.
    \item \textbf{HaluEval (HE)}: hardest dataset by a large margin. Most methods stay around 0.54--0.68 AccTe, with only direction-specific exceptions.
\end{itemize}

An important new finding is the \textbf{directional asymmetry}. For example, on HaluEval, One-For-All is weak in Gemma$\rightarrow$Llama but strong in Llama$\rightarrow$Gemma. Similar asymmetries appear on BBC for linear methods. This suggests that transfer quality depends not only on dataset complexity, but also on which model acts as representation source and which as target.

\subsection{Layer Analysis}
No single layer type (\textit{attn}, \textit{mlp}, \textit{hidden}) is consistently best across all settings.

In-domain transfer with One-For-All is relatively stable across layers on BeliefBank, but layer effects become pronounced in difficult or shifted scenarios. In cross-domain runs, different layers can lead to radically different outcomes under the same train/test pair (e.g., HE$\rightarrow$BBC, Gemma$\rightarrow$Llama: hidden strongly outperforms mlp). This confirms that layer choice is a first-order design decision for universal probing, not a secondary implementation detail.

\subsection{Cross-Domain Analysis}


Transfer toward \textbf{HaluEval} is mostly near chance (mean AccTe around 0.51, mean AUROC around 0.54), regardless of whether training starts from BBF or BBC. The strongest cross-domain results are instead concentrated in \textbf{BeliefBank-to-BeliefBank} transfers, especially BBF$\rightarrow$BBC with Gemma as teacher (up to 0.770 AccTe and 0.961 AUROC).

The cross-domain setting also shows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{strong teacher/student asymmetry}: Gemma$\rightarrow$Llama is better on average than Llama$\rightarrow$Gemma;
    \item \textbf{calibration issues}: several cases exhibit moderate/high AUROC with low AccTe, suggesting ranking quality without a good decision threshold after transfer.
\end{itemize}

\section{Hallucination Mitigation}

This section discusses the two mitigation approaches, TruthX and SLiMTruth, across standard, cross-dataset, and cross-model settings.

\subsection{TruthX}

\subsubsection{Standard Setting}
In the standard setting (Table~\ref{tab:truthx-baseline-standard}), TruthX shows mixed behavior. It is clearly effective on \textbf{HaluEval} ($-34.6\%$ on Gemma, $-50.3\%$ on Llama) and mildly helpful on \textbf{BBC} ($-6.6\%$ and $-6.8\%$).

The critical failure remains \textbf{BBF}, where Llama degrades from 0.0672 to 0.2812 HR ($+318.3\%$), and Gemma also worsens slightly ($+16.3\%$). This confirms strong over-correction when baseline hallucination is already very low.

\subsubsection{Cross-Dataset Transfer}
Cross-dataset TruthX confirms an asymmetric pattern. Transfers from \textbf{BBF/BBC to HE} are often beneficial (up to $-42.4\%$ on Llama), while transfers \textbf{toward BBF} are consistently harmful (up to $+142.4\%$ on Llama).

Transfer toward BBC is moderate and less stable, with small gains in some directions and slight degradation in others.

\subsubsection{Cross-Model Transfer}
With Procrustes alignment, cross-model TruthX is most reliable on \textbf{HaluEval} (Gemma$\rightarrow$Llama: $-38.0\%$, Llama$\rightarrow$Gemma: $-36.7\%$). On BBC, effects are smaller but generally positive; on BBF, results are mixed and remain sensitive to over-correction.

Overall, TruthX is the more transferable mitigation method across architectures, but it is not robust in low-error regimes.

\subsection{SLiMTruth}

\subsubsection{Standard Setting}
In-domain SLiMTruth remains the strongest mitigation setup. It reduces hallucination rate in all tested model/dataset pairs, often substantially (e.g., Llama on BBF: $-89.9\%$; Gemma on BBC: $-59.1\%$; Llama on HE: $-57.9\%$).

\subsubsection{Cross-Dataset Transfer}
Under dataset shift, SLiMTruth is much more fragile. Some directions remain effective (for instance BBF$\rightarrow$HE and BBC$\rightarrow$HE), but transfer toward BBF can fail catastrophically (up to $+1029.7\%$ on Gemma and $+881.8\%$ on Llama for BBC$\rightarrow$BBF).

This confirms that SLiMTruth is highly effective when matched to the training domain, but highly sensitive when correction patterns are applied out-of-domain.

\subsubsection{Cross-Model Transfer}
Cross-model SLiMTruth is mostly ineffective or harmful. Gemma$\rightarrow$Llama improves only on BBC ($-5.8\%$), while all other combinations degrade sharply. Llama$\rightarrow$Gemma degrades on all datasets.

Compared with TruthX, this suggests that layer-specific SLiM interventions are too architecture-dependent to transfer reliably, even with Procrustes alignment.

\subsection{Comparative Analysis: TruthX vs.\ SLiMTruth}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Standard setting}: SLiMTruth is superior and consistently effective.
    \item \textbf{Cross-dataset transfer}: both methods are vulnerable toward BBF; SLiMTruth failures are much larger when transfer is misaligned.
    \item \textbf{Cross-model transfer}: TruthX is clearly more viable, especially on HaluEval.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{The BBF Vulnerability}
A recurring pattern across both methods is the instability on BBF under non-standard transfer. Since BBF starts from very low baseline hallucination rates, interventions trained on noisier domains tend to over-correct and introduce new errors.

\subsubsection{Transferability Hierarchy}
The updated mitigation hierarchy is:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Standard (same model, same dataset)}: strongest regime, with SLiMTruth best overall.
    \item \textbf{Cross-dataset (same model, different dataset)}: viable only for some directions, high risk toward BBF.
    \item \textbf{Cross-model (different model, same dataset)}: currently reliable only for TruthX in selected scenarios (mainly HaluEval).
\end{enumerate}
