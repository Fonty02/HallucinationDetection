\chapter{Conclusions}

This chapter summarizes the main outcomes of the updated experimental campaign and outlines future directions.

\section{Summary of Results}
This work investigated transferable mechanisms for both hallucination \textbf{detection} and \textbf{mitigation} across Gemma and Llama models, using BeliefBankFacts (BBF), BeliefBankConstraints (BBC), and HaluEval (HE).

\subsection{Hallucination Detection}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{One-For-All is the strongest in-domain transfer method on BeliefBank}: it achieves near-perfect tester performance on BBC (up to 0.9947 AccTe, 0.9998 AUROC) and very high results on BBF (up to 0.9628 AccTe, 0.9905 AUROC).
    \item \textbf{No method is universally best on HaluEval}: performance is substantially lower and strongly direction-dependent. In Gemma$\rightarrow$Llama, simpler methods such as RidgeRegressor remain competitive, while in Llama$\rightarrow$Gemma, One-For-All is the best.
    \item \textbf{Directional asymmetry is a core factor}: transfer quality depends heavily on source/target model assignment, not only on method choice.
    \item \textbf{Cross-domain universality is limited}: One-For-All cross-domain transfer is generally weak toward HaluEval (near-chance average), while the strongest cross-domain results are mostly between BBF and BBC.
    \item \textbf{Layer choice matters}: no global winner exists among attn/mlp/hidden, and layer sensitivity increases under domain shift.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Hallucination Mitigation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{SLiMTruth is best in standard settings}: when trained and evaluated on the same model/dataset, it gives the largest and most consistent reductions (up to $-89.9\%$).
    \item \textbf{TruthX is more transferable}: it is less dominant in-domain but more robust across models, with clear cross-model gains on HaluEval (around $-37\%$ in both directions with Procrustes).
    \item \textbf{Cross-dataset transfer is highly asymmetric for both methods}: transfers toward BBF are risky and can become catastrophic, especially for SLiMTruth.
    \item \textbf{BBF vulnerability is recurrent}: low baseline hallucination rates make aggressive steering prone to over-correction.
\end{itemize}

\section{Future Work}
The updated results suggest the following priorities:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Direction-aware transfer strategies}: explicitly model source$\rightarrow$target asymmetry instead of assuming bidirectional equivalence.
    \item \textbf{Post-transfer calibration}: optimize thresholds and score calibration after alignment, especially where AUROC is acceptable but AccTe is low.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive steering strength}: scale mitigation intensity based on estimated baseline risk to avoid over-correction in low-error settings such as BBF.
    \item \textbf{Architecture-aware SLiM transfer}: learn cross-model layer correspondences jointly with modulation parameters, instead of relying on fixed mappings.
    \item \textbf{Richer evaluation protocols}: include additional domains and robustness checks to validate whether discovered truthfulness features remain stable beyond current datasets.
\end{itemize}
